# `plain-language` profile — plain language

A multilingual profile inspired by the public principles of **ISO 24495-1:2023**. Read `references/sources/iso-24495-1.md` before attributing claims to the standard. This reference does not contain the full specification; using it **does not establish conformity**. Plain language is not the same as validated easy read, ASD-STE100 English, or the `feynman` teaching profile.

## Goal

The intended audience must be able to get what they need, find it, understand it, and use it. Clear communication also depends on structure and design. In Explainer, this profile **guides content clarity**, while `--framework`, `--form`, and `--target` continue to determine order, form, and delivery. Do not replace those axes or force an unrequested design.

## Application

1. **Relevant:** identify the question and audience. Start with information that meets their need. If essential information is missing, say so or ask; do not fill gaps. When rewriting, preserve facts, limits, figures, conditions, negations, and warnings even when they are secondary to a quick reading.
2. **Findable:** give headings and labels recognizable names when the target permits it; group only related ideas. In short chat responses, a direct opening sentence is often enough. Do not change the required order of a procedure or break `--keep-structure` without consulting the user.
3. **Understandable:** use familiar words in the requested language, direct sentences, and consistent terms. Explain necessary technical terms when they first appear; do not replace exact names, code, or legal definitions with inaccurate synonyms. Split difficult sentences when doing so does not change their scope.
4. **Usable:** make clear the actions, responsible parties, conditions, and next steps **that the source actually supports**. Distinguish obligations, options, and recommendations. If the source contains no instructions or next steps, do not invent them to complete a template.

## Limits and review

- Do not treat the profile as an instruction to summarize. Removing essential information for brevity makes the result less useful.
- Do not set universal word- or sentence-length limits: the principles adapt to the language and audience. Do not promise that the text is accessible to everyone without validating it with them.
- `--strict` means applying **these available guidelines** more carefully; it does not certify conformity with ISO 24495-1.
- Check against the available context: Can the audience find the answer? Do they understand the terms? Can they use what they learned? Are all data and warnings preserved? Real testing with readers remains pending when it has not been performed.
